Privacy Policy — Sup POS

Last updated: 31 October 2026

Sup POS ("the app") is developed by Sup Digital.

Short version

Your business data stays on your device unless you send it somewhere. The codes you scan, camera images, your catalog, sales and settings are kept in the app's private storage. We, the developer, have no server and never receive any of it. The app has no account system of its own, no advertising and no crash-reporting service of its own.

Where data can go, and only because you set it up: your own Google Drive or OneDrive account if you switch on automatic backup and sign in; your own Sup Booking inbox (a Google Apps Script in your Google account) if you switch on online orders and connect; a printer you choose; and any app you share a receipt or export with.

One thing you cannot switch off: scanner diagnostics. Barcode scanning is done by Google ML Kit, a component built into the app. ML Kit sends Google technical diagnostics about the scanner (your device model, Android version, the app's version, a per-installation identifier and scan performance figures). It does not send camera images or what the codes say. Details are below.

What the app does with your data

Camera. The camera is used only to read QR codes and barcodes. Frames are analysed on your device by a barcode-recognition model bundled inside the app. Camera images are never saved, never uploaded, and never leave your phone.

Pictures you pick. You can pick a picture from your photo album to read a QR code or barcode in it, to give a product a photo, or to set your shop logo. Pictures come through Android's photo picker, so the app can open only the picture you choose and needs no storage permission. A picture scanned for a code is read on your device and not kept. A product photo or logo is shrunk and stored in the app's private storage; it appears on your register, on receipts (the logo) and in your backups, and, if you switch on online orders, as a small copy on your Sup Booking page.

Bluetooth. Bluetooth is used only to send receipts and labels (and, if you switch it on, a cash-drawer pulse) to a printer that you have already paired in your device's own Bluetooth settings. The app does not scan for, track, or collect information about nearby devices, and does not use Bluetooth for location.

Your business data. Your product catalog (including photos, descriptions and ingredient or nutrition notes), stock counts, sales and refund records, held sales, cash-up records and your settings (including store name, logo, GST rate and PayNow details) are stored in the app's private storage on your device.

Internet access. The app uses the internet for exactly four things: Google ML Kit's scanner diagnostics (described below); automatic backup to your Google Drive or OneDrive, only when you have switched it on and signed in; online orders and bookings through your own Sup Booking inbox, only when you have switched them on and connected; and printing to a Wi-Fi or network printer at the address you enter, which is normally on your own shop network.

Scanner diagnostics sent to Google (ML Kit)

The barcode scanner is Google ML Kit's on-device barcode scanning. The recognition itself happens on your phone, but according to Google's ML Kit data disclosure (https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure) ML Kit sends Google, from time to time, for diagnostics and usage analytics:

  • device information: manufacturer, model, Android version and build, and available machine- learning hardware;
  • app information: the app's package name and version;
  • a per-installation identifier, which Google says is not intended to identify a user or a physical device;
  • performance figures such as how long a scan took, the image format and resolution used, the size of the input and output, the ML Kit version, event types and error codes.

Google states that this data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS) and is not passed to third parties. It is handled under Google's Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). The app does not receive or store this data, and there is no setting in the app to turn it off.

Automatic backup to Google Drive or OneDrive (optional, off by default)

If you switch on automatic backup in Settings and sign in, the app copies this device's backup file (your catalog with its photos, sales, cash-ups and stock) and, if you ask for them, sales and catalog spreadsheets, from your phone directly to your own cloud account. The files go over an encrypted connection to Google or Microsoft only; they never pass through any server of ours, and we cannot see them.

  • Google Drive. You sign in with Google's own sign-in screen. The app asks only for permission to see and change the files it creates itself (Google's drive.file access): it makes one folder, "Sup POS", and cannot see anything else in your Drive. Google Play services holds the sign-in; the app keeps your account's email address on this device to show who is signed in.
  • OneDrive. You sign in on Microsoft's own sign-in page in your browser; the app never sees your password. It asks only for access to its own app folder in your OneDrive (under Apps) and for your account's name and email address, to show who is signed in. The access tokens are encrypted with a key kept in your device's secure key store and stay on this device.
  • Several counters. When several of your devices sign in to the same account, each writes only its own files there, and the Shop screen on a device downloads the others' files to add the shop up. Each installation carries a random six-character counter id used only to tell its own files apart; it identifies the installation, not a person.
  • Restore downloads the backup file you pick from your account and deletes the downloaded copy once it has been loaded or you cancel.
  • What Google or Microsoft do with the files is governed by your agreement with them and their privacy policies (https://policies.google.com/privacy, https://privacy.microsoft.com/privacystatement).
  • Stopping. Switching automatic backup off stops all uploads. Sign out in Settings removes the account and its sign-in from this device. It does not withdraw the app's access at Google or Microsoft, because your other counters may still use it. To withdraw it everywhere, use your Google Account (Security ▸ Your connections to third-party apps & services) or your Microsoft account (Privacy ▸ Apps and services). The backup files are yours: delete them in Drive or OneDrive whenever you like.

Online orders and bookings (optional, off by default)

If you switch on online orders and bookings in Settings and connect the app to your Sup Booking inbox, the app exchanges data with that inbox and nothing else. The inbox is a Google Apps Script web app and a Google Sheet that you set up in your own Google account; it runs on Google's servers under your account. We, the developer, do not run it, cannot see it and receive nothing from it. Every exchange uses an encrypted connection.

  • What the app sends to your inbox.
    • Your menu, so your Sup Booking page can show it: your shop's name, address, contact details, currency and logo, your opening hours and what the page takes (pick-up, table orders, bookings), and for each item its name, price, options, description, ingredient or nutrition notes (only if that feature is on), category, how many are left today, and a small photo. It never sends cost prices, barcodes, stock counts beyond "N left today", your sales or any key other than the shop key below.
    • What you do with each order or booking (accept, reject, mark ready, complete or cancel an order; confirm, decline, complete or cancel a booking), with any message to the customer you type.
    • For an online order paid at the register: its receipt number and receipt text, which the customer then sees in their order history. If the inbox cannot be reached, these wait on this device and are sent once it can.
  • What the app receives from your inbox. The orders and bookings your customers placed, including each customer's name and the mobile number and/or email address they gave. The app fetches them every 20 seconds while Home, the register or the Online orders screen is open, and shows them on the Online orders screen. It does not store them in the app's database or in your backups. A paid online order becomes an ordinary sale in your sales records, like any other.
  • The shop key. Connecting uses a connection code that contains a key for your inbox. The app keeps that key on this device only, encrypted with a key held in your device's secure key store and excluded from Android's backup. It is never shown again and is sent only to your inbox, with each request.
  • Contacting customers. The Call, WhatsApp, SMS and email buttons open your phone's own apps with the customer's number or address (and, for a receipt, the receipt text) filled in. Nothing is sent until you press Send in that app, and what happens next is governed by that app's own privacy policy.
  • Your customers' details are yours to look after. You, the shop, collect them through your Sup Booking page and keep them in your Google account, so you are responsible for them under the data protection law that applies to you (in Singapore, the PDPA). Sup Booking includes a privacy notice template for your page; fill it in and have it checked before you take orders. What Google does with data in your account is governed by your agreement with Google and its privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy).
  • Stopping. Switching the feature off stops all contact with the inbox. Disconnect in Settings removes the key from this device. Orders, bookings and the published menu stay in your Google Sheet until you delete them there.

When your business data leaves your device in other ways — only when you choose

  • Sharing a receipt or export. When you export a receipt as a PDF, share it as text, email it, or export your sales as a CSV file, Android's standard share screen opens and the file is handed to whichever app you pick (for example your email or messaging app). What happens to it afterwards is governed by that app's own privacy policy.
  • Opening a scanned link. If you scan a QR code containing a web address and tap "Open in browser", the address is handed to your browser. The app does not fetch or inspect the page.
  • Printing. Receipts and labels go directly to the printer you selected: over Bluetooth, over your network to the printer address you entered, or over a USB cable. If you choose Android printing, the receipt or label is handed to Android's print system and the print service you pick there (for example the Brother or Mopria print service), which handles it under its own privacy policy.
  • Automatic backup to a folder on the phone (optional). Instead of a cloud account you can pick a folder on the phone; the app keeps this device's backup file there and uploads nothing. If you install a folder-sync app such as OneSync and point it at that folder, that app uploads the file under its own and your cloud provider's privacy policies, not ours. When Excel-ready files alongside the backup is on, the folder also receives this device's sales and catalog as spreadsheet files; they hold the same sales records as the backup and deserve the same care.
  • Backup files and catalog exports by hand. When you back up your data or export your catalog, the app writes the file to the location you pick in Android's file picker. Keep backup files private: they contain your sales records.
  • Importing. When you restore a backup or import a catalog spreadsheet, the app reads only the single file you pick.
  • Keyboard-style barcode scanners. A USB or Bluetooth scanner you connect acts as a keyboard; the app reads the codes it types while the register is open and nothing else.

Android's own backup

If you have Android's automatic backup enabled, your app settings (including the online-orders settings and your inbox's address) and shop logo may be included in your Google account backup under Google's own privacy policy. Your sales ledger database (which also holds your product photos), cloud sign-ins, the online-orders shop key, receipts still waiting to reach the inbox and the counter id are excluded from Android's automatic backup and from device-to-device transfer.

Permissions summary

Permission Why
Camera Reading QR codes and barcodes, on-device only
Bluetooth (connect) Sending receipts and labels to a printer you already paired
Bluetooth / Bluetooth admin (Android 11 and older only) The pre-Android-12 equivalents of the above, used to reach the paired printer
Nearby devices: local network (Android 17 and later) Reaching the Wi-Fi / LAN printer you set up on your shop's network; asked for only if you choose such a printer. The app does not scan your network or look for other devices on it
Internet Google ML Kit's scanner diagnostics; backup to your own Google Drive or OneDrive when you switch it on; online orders and bookings through your own Sup Booking inbox when you switch them on; printing to a network printer you set up
Network state, run at startup, keep awake, foreground service Granted automatically; used by Android's background-job library to run the optional automatic backup on time

USB printers are reached through Android's own USB permission prompt, shown when you first print to one. The app requests no location, contacts, storage, microphone or account permissions.
